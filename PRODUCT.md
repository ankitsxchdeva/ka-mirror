# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Mechanical keyboard hobbyists and artisan keycap collectors. Typical context:
at a desk in the evening with Discord, a marketplace thread, or a photo of an
unidentified cap open in another window. The job is identification and
reference: "which maker made this", "what colorways exist of this sculpt",
"link this exact colorway to someone". Sessions are short, frequent, and
lookup-shaped. Power users navigate by keyboard and expect search to be fast.

## Product Purpose

A read-only static mirror of the Keycap Archivist community database
(238 makers, ~69k catalogued colorways, regenerated daily from the makers' own
Google Docs). It exists because the original site's server-dependent features
kept breaking; the mirror keeps the archive browsable with zero moving parts.
Success: a collector finds and shares the exact cap they mean in under thirty
seconds.

## Brand Personality

Archival, precise, utilitarian. The tone of a well-kept card catalog: the
interface recedes, the specimen photography and the data carry everything.
Three words: dense, exact, quiet.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI dark theme: brown/amber gradients, rounded card grids, glassy
  blurs, hero stat blocks.
- Marketing-site energy of any kind: this is a tool, not a landing page.
- Linear/dashboard cosplay: no purple-tinted indigo, no cmd-palette theater
  beyond what search genuinely needs.

## Design Principles

- The photograph is the payload. Chrome shrinks so specimen images and counts
  read first.
- Density is respect: a collector scanning 365 colorways wants rows and grids
  that fit the screen, not padding.
- Everything addressable: every maker, sculpt, and colorway has a stable id
  and a copyable link, surfaced in the UI like accession numbers.
- Keyboard-first lookup: search reachable from anywhere with "/", results
  navigable without the mouse.
- Earned familiarity: standard table/list affordances, no invented widgets.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA contrast in both themes. Full keyboard operability (search, lightbox,
pinning). prefers-reduced-motion collapses all transitions. Alt text on
specimen images. No information conveyed by color alone.
